Design Research

Instructor Zihao Zhang, Ph.D., Associate Professor Email zzhang@ccny.cuny.edu Semester TBD — City College of New York, Spitzer School of Architecture
Overview

Design Research asks how designers produce knowledge, and how that knowledge earns support. The seminar treats research as a practice with its own craft: a specific question, a method chosen on purpose, evidence gathered from real sources, and an argument a reader can follow.

Two commitments run through it. The first is that making is a method. Collage, models, scores, mapping, typological catalogs and measured drawing each generate knowledge the written account cannot reach — drawing a thing forces you to respond to it and to ask the next question. The second is that theory is also a method, supplying the frameworks and vocabulary that let a project join a larger conversation. Alongside both, students learn where the material actually comes from: federal and local geographic data, historical map collections, public-domain image archives, patents and scholarly databases.

Running underneath both is a third commitment: research is never neutral. A framework decides what you will be able to see, a dataset records what someone decided was worth measuring, and an archive holds what an institution thought worth keeping. Students learn to say where they are asking from, and to read a source for what it leaves out.

The seminar closes on the proposal. Research that stays private changes nothing, so students practice writing it for a reader — plain language for knowledgeable non-experts, a clear gap, a realistic work plan, and an argument for why the work matters beyond the discipline.

Four of its nine parts work the ground underneath a method. Asking the question argues for the how question over the why: a why needs a cause isolated from its rivals, a what stops at description, a how names a process whose parts you can go and look at. Why theory matters treats a framework as equipment with a range and a blind spot. Paradigms and incommensurability reads Kuhn for what he actually claimed. How disciplines differ tours what each field counts as evidence, then turns to working across those boundaries — trading zones, boundary objects, and the difference between multi-, inter- and transdisciplinary work.

The lectures come in two registers. What is Research is the general version, usable with students from any discipline; What is Research for Designers takes the same ground through drawing, models, mapping and typological catalogs.

What is Research

  • Research is the pursuit of new knowledge through the process of discovery. Purdue University
  • Two grammars of the word: Open English Wordnet
    • Noun: enquiry, inquiry, a search for knowledge
    • Verb: attempt to find out in a systematic and scientific manner
  • Research is the systematic and intentional process of asking questions, gathering evidence, interpreting data, and generating knowledge — a way of making sense of the world, whether that world is social, ecological, technological or historical. ChatGPT

How to do research?

  • What do you want to study?
    • Be specific. You cannot just say you want to study pumpkins — what about pumpkins?
  • Why do you want to study it, and why now?
    • What is the impact of the topic — climate change, social injustice, democracy?
  • How are you going to study it? What is your method?
    • How do you collect, analyze and interpret information? Qualitative, quantitative, design research?
    • Through historical documents, critical theory, drawing, mapping?
  • What is your outcome?

What is a Method

  • Quantitative and qualitative are the traditional way scientists talk about method.
  • The standard split:
    • Quantitative — numerical data, statistical analysis; objective, measurable, expressed in numbers.
    • Qualitative — non-numerical data (words, images, sounds); subjective experience and attitudes, via observation and interview; detailed description and new insight.
  • Method is simply how you study something — so explain how you are going to study it.
    • Making
    • Theorizing
  • Theory becomes a method to study something.
    • Posthumanism as a framework to study pumpkins; “thing-power” to understand edible plants; watermelon through a racial or colonial lens.
  • Combine methods and you have a new method.

Making as a Method

  • When you draw something you are forced to respond to it — and forced to ask more questions.
  • What do you do, and why? Use verbs.
    • Diagram root systems to understand how a root system grows over time.
    • Combine different typologies to create new forms of public space.
    • Analyze historical trade images to understand the relationship between China and the West.
  • Post-rationalization is allowed — you will often know what you were doing only after you did it.
  • It should be fun.

Theorizing as a Method

  • What is theory?
    • A well-substantiated explanation of an aspect of the natural world that can incorporate laws, hypotheses and facts. American Museum of Natural History
    • A supposition or system of ideas intended to explain something, especially one based on general principles independent of the thing to be explained. Oxford Dictionary
  • Why do we need theory?
    • Theories provide frameworks and vocabularies to study with; they provide the language to create discourse and generate knowledge within.
  • Theory bridges, mediates and reconciles thought and action. Beth Meyer

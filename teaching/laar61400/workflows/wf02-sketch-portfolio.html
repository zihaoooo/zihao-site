Hybrid Drawing
Scan · Hybridize · Diagram · Lay Out
Length1 session
SheetsPlan 11"×17"; others any size

Learning Objectives
  • How hand linework and digital media share one drawing
  • Linked files: the Photoshop → Illustrator → InDesign pipeline a firm uses for a presentation deck
AssignmentA presentation deck of your WF06 project, laid out in InDesign from linked files and exported as one PDF: hand drawings scanned and hybridized in Photoshop, and the plan on an 11"×17" page over a toned-down satellite base with diagrams drawn in Illustrator.

01 Scan Your Hand Drawings Scanner
Scan each WF06 drawing you want in the deck — the plan, sections, the perspective — in color at 300 dpi. Drawings can be any size; keep one file per drawing.
  • Scan in color even for pen drawings; you correct the paper tone in the next step
  • No scanner: photograph each sheet flat in even daylight, phone parallel to the paper. Adobe Scan or Microsoft Lens work.
02 White Balance Each Scan Photoshop
Make the paper read as clean white and the lines as true black. Add a Levels adjustment layer and click the paper with its white-point eyedropper; this removes the yellow or blue cast in one move.
  • Drag the black slider in until the linework is solid, and the white slider until paper texture drops out
  • Still a color cast? Use the gray-point eyedropper on a neutral area, or Filter → Camera Raw Filter → Temperature and Tint
  • Straighten and crop with C (Crop) before you add anything
03 Hybridize the Drawings Photoshop
Collage other material into your sections and perspective: a photographed canopy set on your hand-drawn trunks, a ground texture, sky, figures. Put the scan on top and set its blending mode to Multiply so your lines stay in front and the paper drops out.
  • Use layer masks, not the eraser — you can always paint a mask back
  • Clip a Hue/Saturation or Curves adjustment to each asset (Alt-click between layers) so it matches the rest
  • Let the hand linework lead: keep assets partial, and leave some areas as pure drawing
  • Optional: site photos or a Hockney-style joiner can be one of your assets
04 Set the Plan on a Satellite Base Photoshop
The plan is the one sheet with a fixed size: 11"×17" landscape at 300 dpi. Place the WF06 aerial under your plan scan, add a Black & White adjustment clipped to the aerial, and lower the aerial’s opacity until it reads as context and your linework leads.
  • The aerial is on the WF06 page, same frame and scale as the base plan
  • Line the two up with Ctrl T (Free Transform) and scale uniformly, so the plan stays at 1"=20'
  • Start the aerial around 30–50% opacity and adjust by eye
05 Save Layered PSDs Photoshop
Save every drawing as a layered PSD in a Links folder next to where your InDesign file will live. These are your working files; the deck links to them.
  • File → Save As → Photoshop (.psd), layers kept
  • Name files by drawing: plan.psd, section-a.psd, perspective.psd
06 Diagram the Plan in Illustrator Illustrator
Open a 17×11 in artboard and File → Place the plan PSD with Link checked. On layers above it, draw the diagrams the plan needs: movement arrows, zones, labels, a north arrow, and a scale bar.
  • Lock the PSD layer so you only draw on the diagram layers
  • Need to change the drawing? Select it and click Edit Original in the Links panel; the update comes back to Illustrator
  • Draw the scale bar from the 1"=20' base: 1 inch on the artboard is 20 feet
  • Save as .ai with Create PDF Compatible File on, so InDesign can place it
07 Lay Out the Deck in InDesign InDesign
Build the deck with as many pages as your project needs. Make the plan page 11"×17" landscape; size the other pages to suit the drawings. Ctrl D places the plan .ai and the PSDs as links. Add a title and short captions, then export one PDF.
  • Change a PSD and the .ai and the deck both update; the Links panel flags any file that moved
  • File → Export → Adobe PDF (Print), and post it to Miro
  • File → Package collects the InDesign file and every link into one folder when you hand the deck off

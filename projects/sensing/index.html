Prototyping is the method, and also the argument. The sensing kits are open-hardware assemblies — an Arduino, a GPS breakout, a microSD logger, and a stack of environmental sensors — small enough to sit in a palm and cheap enough to hand out. Each round of soldering, field-testing, and rebuilding changes what the instrument can ask, so the design question and the technical question stay entangled. Because the device is legible and reproducible, measuring becomes something a resident can do, rather than something done to a neighborhood.

The responsive work takes the same sensor logic into space, turning readings that are usually invisible into something a body registers directly. Instrument and installation are two halves of one practice: one asks what the environment is doing, the other asks what it feels like to be inside it.

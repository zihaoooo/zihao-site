Hundred Quarters (百刻)

2026 · Digital Art · Landscape Simulation · Computer Vision

Output
  • Hundred Quarters (百刻) — YouTube channel, one scroll per solar term (节气) and per five-day season (候)
Tools
  • Claude Code — the coding partner: engine, pipeline and analysis written in conversation
  • Python, NumPy / CuPy — the scroll renderer, on the GPU
  • NVIDIA Warp — wind and plant stems as physical rods
  • Faust / DawDreamer — generative process music, tuned to the season
  • Depth Anything V2 — monocular depth, to measure the scroll’s space

Hundred Quarters is a landscape that runs as a program. Each video is one continuous handscroll, drawn frame by frame from a score: the land and water, the hills, the plants and the animals are placed by a planner, painted as flat cut paper, and carried past a camera through one day’s light. Nothing is filmed and no image is generated by a model; every scene is composed, and every rule is written.

The rules come from the classical calendar and its texts. The year is cut into the twenty-four solar terms (二十四节气) and seventy-two five-day seasons (候), each with its own mode and key after the Monthly Ordinances (《月令》), its own palette, and the plant or animal its season names. Durations come from the time units of the Mahāsāṅghika Vinaya (《摩诃僧祇律》): a breath (息) of 7.2 seconds, a quarter (刻) of 14:24, a double hour (时辰) of exactly 2:00:00. The sound is process music built on that breath, and it shares the score’s timeline with the picture, so the scroll reaches its shore as the bell rings.

The research question is space. A Chinese handscroll makes depth out of reserved blank (留白) and mist between flat planes, with no perspective floor. A video scroll has to move, and motion asks how far each plane is. The project reads its own frames with a monocular depth model, compares them with photographs of the karst landscapes they derive from, and rebuilds the renderer around one real camera, so the flat planes keep their painterly look while they pass at the rates a lens would see.

The project is also a test of who gets to use these tools. Monocular depth models, GPU physics, audio DSP languages and scientific Python are open source and free, but they are built by and for programmers: command lines, environments, APIs, papers. Working with Claude Code, a designer can reach them by describing intent in plain language, reading what comes back, and judging the result as a designer would, by the still, the clip and the ear. The engine here was written that way, in conversation, over weeks; the design decisions stay the designer’s, and code becomes a medium rather than a barrier.

The latest piece. Every video on the channel goes public at the moment its season begins.

One camera

A level pinhole camera 0.58 m above the ground, its horizon at 0.795 of the frame, a 12° vertical field (about a 114 mm lens): a cat’s eye from far off. On the ground a thing’s distance follows from its foot alone, Z = h f / (y − yhorizon); everything slides across the screen at a rate proportional to 1 / Z. The cat, 0.30 m tall, walks 34.5 m off; the far hills stand 3 km away and drift a hundredth as fast. Below, each layer’s slide over one breath, drawn to scale against the width of the frame.

Composition: the score

Every piece begins as a score (score.json): a timeline of sections, chords, pulse cells, bells and the scenes the scroll passes through. Sound and picture are both performances of it, so a bell, a change of chord and the arrival at a shore are one event, written once. The idea follows the landscape architect Lawrence Halprin, who scored movement through landscape (his Motation notation and the RSVP Cycles) and treated a score as a set of instructions that generates an experience over time rather than as a fixed product. Here the score is read by machines, and the landscape it generates is both heard and travelled through.

The music is generative process music after Steve Reich’s Music for 18 Musicians: a cycle of chords stated as a pulsing cantus, each opened into its own section, notes added and dropped one breath at a time. Its clock is the breath: a pulse of two blinks (瞬, 0.72 s, about 83 BPM), a bar of ten that is one breath (息, 7.2 s). Pitch follows the Monthly Ordinances (《月令》): each month gives the mode and the pitch pipe that sets the home tone, so late autumn sounds in the shang mode (商) on B♭ and early winter in the yu mode (羽) on B. The voices are tuned noise, pink noise rung through narrow resonators, so from a distance the harmony is still weather; a temple bell marks each turn between work and rest.

Reading depth with a model

The finished frame (left), the depth the renderer itself knows, each plane at its camera distance (centre), and Depth Anything V2 reading the same frame (right). The model finds the near grass, the cat and the ground plane, and sees the hills as cut-outs a little in front of the sky: it reads the paper as paper.
